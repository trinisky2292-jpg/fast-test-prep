## 1. Official FAST Item TYPES (response formats)

The real test is computer-based; the paper sample mirrors the same item logic. These are the
response formats Florida uses for Grade 6:

| Type | What it is | How Florida phrases it | Grade 6 examples seen |
|------|-----------|------------------------|-----------------------|
| **Multiple choice (MC)** | One correct answer, 4 options A–D | "Which value represents...?", "What does the word ___ mean...?", "Which sentence...?" | Math item 4 (unit rate); Reading items 1,2,3,8,9,10,11,12 |
| **Multiselect ("select all")** | 2+ correct answers, 5–6 options. Number to pick is often stated. | "Select all the questions that...", "Select two statements that...", "Select all the values that are equivalent to..." | Math items 1, 10; Reading item 4 |
| **Two-part / multi-part (EBSR)** | Part A asks for an answer; Part B asks for the evidence/expression that supports Part A. Sometimes a single stem with two bracketed dropdowns. | "This question has two parts. First, answer Part A. Then, answer Part B." / "Which sentence from the passage supports the answer in Part A?" | Math items 3, 8, 9; Reading items 6, 7 |
| **Equation editor (constructed response, numeric)** | Student types a number/expression into a shaded box. No options. | "Write your response in the shaded box below." Key adds: "Other correct responses: any equivalent value." | Math items 2, 5, 9 |
| **Dropdown / inline choice** | Bracketed menus embedded in a sentence; pick one per blank. | "Select words or phrases to complete the statements..." / "For each blank, fill in the bubble before the number or phrase that is correct." Sentence shows `[ A 2  B 4  C 5  D 9 ]`. | Math items 6, 8 |
| **Grid / matrix match (multi-select table)** | A table; fill one bubble per row to classify each detail into a category column. | "Fill in the bubbles to show whether each detail..." / "Fill in bubbles to match each point with its coordinates." | Math item 7 (coordinates); Reading items 5, 13 (theme/purpose) |
| **Hot text** | Select sentence(s)/word(s) directly inside the passage (CBT only; the paper version surfaces as a multiple-choice/multiselect of quoted sentences). | "Select the detail from Passage 2 where the author appeals to the emotions..." | Reading item 11 |
| **Drag-and-drop** | CBT format; student drags tiles into targets (number line, table, sequence). On paper it renders as a labeled multiple-choice (e.g., choose the correct number line). | Number-line / plotting items. | Math item 3 Part A (number line) |
| **Table / grid-in** | Type values into a small table or shaded boxes (e.g., box-plot min/max, two-part numeric). | "Write your response in the shaded box below." (one box per part) | Math item 9 (shortest / longest from box plot) |

> Note on paper vs CBT: drag-and-drop, hot-text, and equation-editor are native to the
> computer-based test. The official **paper** sample materials present the same cognitive task
> using bubbles/shaded boxes. Build both: our digital app can use the true CBT interactions,
> but the question STEMS should read identically to the state's wording below.

## 2. How Florida phrases question STEMS (copy these patterns)

### Math stems
- Lead with a short real-world setup, then ask in one clean sentence.
  - "On Monday, 5/8 inch of rain fell in 2/3 hour. **Which value represents the unit rate, in inches per hour?**"
  - "A grocery store... **Which statement is true?**"
- Constructed response: "An expression is shown. (22.4)(2.65) **What is the value of the expression? Write your response in the shaded box below.**"
- Evaluate/substitute: "**Evaluate the expression** c + 3(c/d) • d, **where** c = -4 **and** d = 2."
- Multiselect: "**Select all the** questions that Mr. Balboa can ask that will generate numerical data with variation."
- Two-part header (verbatim): "**This question has two parts.**" then "**Part A**" / "**Part B**".
- Dropdown instruction (verbatim): "**Select words or phrases to complete the statements**... For each box, fill in the bubble before the word or phrase that is correct."
- Inline dropdown rendering: "Kate will use _____ [ A 2  B 4  C 5  D 9 ] containers..."
- Units are always stated explicitly ("in inches per hour", "in centimeters (cm)", "in degrees Celsius (°C)").
- Numbers use real fractions/mixed numbers (5/8, 1 1/2, -4 1/2), decimals, and negatives.
- Names are diverse and neutral (Mr. Balboa, Ms. Becker, Ms. Garcia, Kate).

### Reading (ELA) stems
- Always anchor to the text and cite the location:
  - "**In paragraph 2**, how does comparing the sea to a rain forest... add to the meaning of the passage?"
  - "**Read this sentence from the passage.** 'It brightens the room with wonder.' **(paragraph 3)** What tone is created...?"
- Vocabulary-in-context: "**What does the word _relishes_ mean as it is used in the sentence?**"
  - Morphology variant (gives roots): "The word 'unanimity' is a combination of the Latin words _unus_ (one) and _animus_ (mind, spirit). **Based on this, what is the meaning of...?**"
- Theme/central idea: "**Which theme is developed in the passage?**" then Part B "**Which sentence from the passage supports the theme in Part A?**"
- Evidence pairing is the signature ELA move: Part A = claim, Part B = "Choose the sentence from paragraph 10 that supports the answer in Part A."
- Cross-text (paired passages): "**How does Passage 2 approach... differently than Passage 1?**"; classify by "Passage 1 / Passage 2 / Both Passages" grid.
- Author's purpose / craft: "Which **feature, if added** to Passage 1, would be useful...?"; "Select the detail... where the **author appeals to the emotions** of the audience."
- Quotes are reproduced verbatim with the paragraph number in parentheses: `"..." (paragraph 3)`.
- Passages: one **literary** (story/poem) + paired **informational** texts (often a primary-source pair), each with author byline and copyright/source line.

## 3. Answer-key conventions (mirror in explanations)
- Each correct option is explained as: "**Option C: This answer is correct.** The passage describes..."
- Numeric constructed-response keys add: "**Other correct responses: any equivalent value.**"
- Explanations are 1–2 sentences, tie directly back to the text/computation, and quote the
  supporting evidence. Keep ours the same: concise, evidence-anchored, verify-the-answer style.

---

## 4. Rules of thumb for our AI generator
1. Math: setup sentence → single explicit question → state units → use fractions/decimals/negatives like the state does.
2. Always offer the FAST type mix, not just MC: ~40% MC, plus multiselect, two-part EBSR, dropdown, grid-match, and equation-editor.
3. For every two-part item, Part B must demand the *evidence/expression* for Part A.
4. ELA: never ask a question that can be answered without the passage; always cite paragraph numbers and quote verbatim.
5. ELA reading sets: pair a literary text with informational/paired primary sources; include vocabulary-in-context and an author's-purpose/cross-text item per set.
6. Distractors are plausible near-misses (e.g., 4 1/8 = 4.125 vs correct 4 7/40 = 4.175; 16/15 vs 15/16).
7. Keep names neutral and varied; keep contexts everyday and grade-appropriate.
8. Tag every item to a B.E.S.T. benchmark (MA.6.* / ELA.6.*) and state difficulty.
